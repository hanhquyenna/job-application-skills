# Cover letters that sound like a person and prove a fit

## What the evidence says

A cover letter is a short decision document. It should answer two questions the CV cannot answer quickly: **why this role and employer, and what can this person do for us?** University career centers and government career services agree on four practical requirements: tailor the letter to the exact vacancy, explain the employer connection with a concrete detail, use a small number of relevant examples, and keep the writing short and error free.

The sources do not support stuffing every keyword into the letter. They support using the employer's language where it accurately describes your experience, then proving the words with a real task, method, audience, and result. This distinction explains why a letter can contain the right terms and still feel like buzzword writing.

There is also a limit to claims about "successful" letters. Most published examples are teaching models, not letters with audited hiring outcomes. The strongest outcome evidence I found is a 2025/2026 academic study of roughly five million Freelancer applications. Before an AI writing tool was introduced, textual alignment with the job post predicted callbacks. After the tool became available, tailoring increased, but the relationship between tailoring and callbacks fell by about 51%; employers shifted toward harder-to-fake signals such as prior work reviews. More time editing the AI draft was associated with better outcomes. The practical lesson is to use AI for structure and checking, then make the final letter yours.

## How a strong letter works

1. **Read the vacancy as a problem statement.** Mark the two responsibilities that matter most, the tools or outputs named repeatedly, and any eligibility constraint. For ING eFX Sales, the central work is analysing FX data and turning it into reports or decisions for sales, trading, technology, and clients.
2. **Research one real employer detail.** Use the employer's own job page, product page, annual report, or recent announcement. Do not write generic praise. ING's posting names institutional and corporate clients, electronic execution, and products such as spot, forwards, swaps, NDFs, options, and algorithmic order strategies.
3. **Build an evidence map.** For each top requirement, record one experience with: the situation or stakes, what you personally did, how you did it, and the output or result. Use only facts the CV or a separately confirmed anecdote supports.
4. **Write the opening around the employer.** Name the role and connect the researched detail to the problem the internship is meant to solve. One sentence should be impossible to reuse for another company.
5. **Use two proof paragraphs.** Each should be a small case study, not a CV bullet in sentence form. Start with the problem or decision, describe the action and method, then show what changed or what the work enabled.
6. **Close with the day-one contribution.** State what you could help the team do and ask for a conversation. Avoid a separate paragraph about what you hope to learn.
7. **Edit for human language.** Remove abstract nouns, inflated claims, forced three-part lists, repeated sentence openings, and any phrase that could survive swapping in another company. Keep the exact technical term when it is needed for truthful matching.
8. **Run the factual check.** Verify every company, title, date, metric, tool, product name, and claim against the CV/JD snapshot. Check that the final PDF text is selectable and that the letter stays on one page.

A useful internal test is: **requirement → evidence → method → result → contribution**. If a sentence has only a requirement word and no evidence, it is probably a buzzword sentence.

## What the model examples teach

- **Stanford's job-listing sample** names the position, identifies a contact, gives a compact academic/experience match, and closes by asking for a conversation. It is a useful anatomy model, not proof that the sample itself led to a hire. [Stanford sample PDF](https://careered.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22801/files/media/file/resume-and-cover-letter-examples.pdf)
- **Prospects' logistics sample** links a degree, data work, retail/warehouse situations, and specific outcomes. It also explicitly warns readers to use the sample for structure and write an original letter. [Prospects sample](https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/cover-letters/sample-cover-letter/)
- **New Zealand Careers' healthcare sample** demonstrates the sequence: role/reference, why the applicant is writing, a short history, relevant evidence, employer reason, and a conversation request. Its checklist is useful for internships because it forces the writer to name what they can contribute. [Careers New Zealand example](https://www.careers.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/Making-your-Career-Kakala-Applying-for-jobs-CVs-cover-letters-and-more-Interactive-2022.pdf)
- **Harvard's 2026 guide** gives the most practical modern pattern: a specific opening, two examples, an action/method/result explanation, one page, and human editing after any AI assistance. [Harvard 2026 guide](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/blog/2026/08/10/how-to-write-a-cover-letter-that-actually-gets-you-hired-in-2026/)
- **UC San Diego's example** is valuable because the guide marks a generic mission statement as insufficient and tells writers to show qualifications through specific examples. [UCSD guide](https://career.ucsd.edu/resources/guides/cover-letter.html)

## Application to Kien's ING letter

The first version used too many abstract nouns: “financial outputs,” “survive scrutiny,” “underlying numbers,” “find opportunities,” and “clearer electronic trading decisions.” Those phrases sound plausible but do not show a person doing work. The stronger version describes a senior decision over $100M, a DCF model, changed financing assumptions, SQL data, 10+ reports, 25+ client profiles, and the checks used in the Python project.

For this role, keep only terms that earn their place: **eFX Sales, FX data, Excel, Power BI, SQL, Python, DCF, scenario analysis, and electronic execution**. Do not claim FX trading experience that the CV does not prove. The letter should show that you can analyse financial data, check the inputs, explain the result, and work with the desk. That is a truthful bridge into eFX without pretending you have already traded FX.

The revised working formula is:

> ING task: turn electronic-FX data into a usable desk decision.  
> FLC evidence: model large investment decisions and test financing assumptions.  
> BIDV evidence: turn raw financial data into reports and summaries.  
> Python evidence: check model outputs before trusting the sensitivity result.  
> Day-one contribution: analyse, report, check, and explain.

That is how the letter works. It does not win by containing every keyword. It wins when the reader can see the employer's problem, recognise evidence that you have handled a nearby problem, and understand what you could do in their team immediately.
