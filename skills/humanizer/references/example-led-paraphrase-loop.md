# Example-led paraphrase loop

Use this module when the user wants the draft improved by learning from many successful public examples and iterating until the writing meets the user's quality bar.

## Purpose

Public examples calibrate the writing choices. They do not supply personal facts or distinctive sentences. Extract the choices that recur in strong examples, then rebuild the user's draft from the locked evidence and approved voice.

## Source handling

1. Select at least three relevant example families: finance internship, consulting internship, and candid applicant or actual-letter examples when available.
2. For each source, record the opening trigger, first-person reaction, story signal, decision, action, consequence, transition, close, sentence-length range, and useful verbs.
3. Label each source as institutional guidance, public sample, practitioner advice, applicant letter, or claimed-success anecdote. A claimed interview or hiring outcome is not verified success.
4. Paraphrase the source's craft. Do not copy distinctive wording, paragraph sequences, or more than a short excerpt allowed by copyright limits.
5. Keep a wording ledger with three columns: source wording pattern, safe abstract function, and Kien-approved replacement. Never let the source column overwrite the candidate evidence.

## Iteration protocol

Begin with a first draft from the source-of-truth ledger. Preserve the user's paragraph order unless they request a change.

### Pass 1: phrase layer

Change only word choices and short connecting phrases. Prefer the verbs and concrete nouns that recur in the examples, but use them only when they describe a supported action. Remove generic application phrases, abstract nouns, and claims that merely announce fit. Run a factual diff.

### Pass 2: sentence-flow layer

Change one sentence at a time. Test four shapes from the examples:

- event → reaction → check or choice
- signal → uncertainty → action
- task → audience → decision
- company detail → candidate question → role contribution

Vary sentence length naturally. Keep the writer's uncertainty, caveat, and technical terms. Do not add artificial mistakes or fragments.

### Pass 3: paragraph-flow layer

Test at least three orders against the JD:

- company trigger → primary story → supporting proof → close
- primary story → company trigger → supporting proof → close
- candid boundary → primary story → company-specific application → close

Select the order that makes the reader understand the candidate's reason before the final paragraph. Keep the strongest story first when it carries the personal motivation.

### Pass 4: reader and browser feedback

Run the swap test, second-CV test, read-aloud test, interview-continuation test, and factual diff. If the user authorized a browser detector, scan the exact draft. Classify each highlight as evidence gap, structural symmetry, wording, vocabulary, or detector noise. Revise the smallest affected unit and rescan.

When the browser marks text green or as human-like, treat that wording and sentence rhythm as a preferred candidate for the next draft. First check it against the source-of-truth ledger. Keep it only if it states a supported fact and fits the user's voice. Do not import a green greeting, sign-off, or generic sentence merely because the detector labels it human-like.

### Pass 5: comparison and selection

Keep a table of every draft, source pattern used, changed layer, quality score, browser result, highlighted sentences, and reason for retaining or rejecting it. Select the strongest editorial version, not automatically the lowest detector score. If two versions are close, prefer the one with the more concrete decision, less generic commentary, and closer match to the user's samples.

## Continuation rule

Do not stop merely because a detector score stays unchanged or because the first set of rewrites did not pass. Continue with a genuinely different example family or a different transformation layer. Stop only when the quality gates pass, when the source-of-truth lacks the requested detail, or when two consecutive iterations make no legitimate improvement. State which condition caused the stop. Never claim a detector pass without a visible result.

## Kien-specific guardrails

- Keep the approved facts: FLC's 64% IRR and 18–25% comparison, checking with colleagues, one-page board reports, bid and lending limits, floating-rate sensitivity, BIDV's reports, $40M+ portfolios, 25+ profiles, Excel, and Power BI.
- Keep the agreed application order: company-specific event first, direct connection to the primary FLC story second, supporting evidence after that, and a short close.
- Keep Kien's direct first-person choices where supplied: “I stopped,” “I could not explain the gap,” “I checked the calculation,” and “before the documents went to the bank.”
- Do not invent the cause of the IRR gap, an outcome of the bank discussion, a personal ING connection, or a client result.
