# Cover Letters That Add a Person, Not a Second CV

## Research question

Why can a technically accurate, tailored cover letter still feel polished, generic, or like a rewritten CV? What should change when the applicant has real moments of judgment but limited outcome detail?

## Finding

The repeated guidance is not simply “tell a story.” It is more precise: the CV supplies the claim, while the letter supplies the missing context that lets a reader see how the person thinks. The useful unit is a small decision under a real condition: an unexpected number, a deadline, an unclear assumption, a stakeholder who needed a different explanation, or a problem that forced the applicant to choose what to check first.

A letter feels like a second CV when each sentence follows this pattern: company → task → tool → metric. It feels human when the paragraph follows: situation → signal that something was wrong or mattered → choice → action → consequence or working rule → relevance to this role. The consequence can be an outcome, a decision, or a lesson that is explicitly tied to the next job. It must not be invented.

## What the sources agree on

1. **Add context instead of repeating the résumé.** The University of Victoria says a cover letter should avoid repeating the résumé and use solid examples to provide context. UC Davis describes the letter as a personalised statement that tells the applicant’s story. Oregon State and Northeastern Illinois use the same distinction: elaborate on what the résumé cannot show, rather than restating it. ([University of Victoria](https://www.uvic.ca/career-services/find-work/apply-for-jobs/cover-letters/index.php); [UC Davis](https://careercenter.ucdavis.edu/resumes-and-materials/cover-letters); [Oregon State](https://career.oregonstate.edu/sites/career.oregonstate.edu/files/2024-08/what_goes_in_a_cover_letter.pdf); [Northeastern Illinois](https://www.neiu.edu/sites/default/files/inline_files/Guide%20to%20Writing%20Effective%20Cover%20Letters.docx.pdf))

2. **Use one or two specific stories, not a catalogue of evidence.** Penn explicitly recommends specific stories, examples, or anecdotes and says the reader already has the résumé. Boston College recommends a brief story from the résumé that shows the relevant skills without repeating it. Dartmouth asks applicants to present their most relevant story and, where useful, explain what first sparked their interest. ([University of Pennsylvania](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/cover-letters/); [Boston College](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/offices/studentaffairs/sites/careers/prepare/cover-letter.html); [Dartmouth](https://careerdesign.dartmouth.edu/resources/cover-letter-guide/))

3. **Show the skill in context.** Penn says the persuasive part is showing how an experience developed the skill, not merely asserting that the skill exists. UC San Diego says to show, rather than tell, qualifications with specific examples. The University of Virginia similarly recommends expanding one or two relevant experiences instead of reciting the whole background. ([Penn](https://careerservices.upenn.edu/channels/cover-letters/); [UC San Diego](https://career.ucsd.edu/resources/guides/cover-letter.html); [University of Virginia](https://career.virginia.edu/cover-letters/))

4. **A story needs a decision point.** STAR/SAR/CSARR guidance from the UK National Careers Service, Carleton, UMass Amherst, Waterloo, Highline College, and the University of Colorado Denver all place situation or context before action and result. The practical implication is that “built a model” is incomplete until the reader knows what made the model necessary and what the applicant chose to do. ([National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice/the-star-method); [Carleton](https://carleton.ca/career/job-search/resume-cover-letter-cv-guides/sar-statements/); [UMass Amherst](https://sbspathways.umass.edu/sbs-cover-letter-guide/); [Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/career-development/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/4.-how-to-make-cover-letters-less-painful-rhythm-oct-7-copy.pdf); [Highline College](https://documents.highline.edu/studentemployment/STAR-Method.pdf); [University of Colorado Denver](https://clas.ucdenver.edu/writing-center/sites/default/files/attached-files/csarr_for_cover_letters_personal_statements.pdf))

5. **The letter should answer the employer’s problem, then show how the applicant works.** Time’s guidance says to identify the employer’s pain point, avoid regurgitating the résumé, keep anecdotes short, and spend most of the letter showing what hiring the applicant would do for the company. Georgetown similarly says to focus on contribution rather than what the opportunity will do for the applicant. ([Time](https://time.com/4206709/cover-letter-tips/); [Georgetown](https://careercenter.georgetown.edu/major-career-guides/resumes-cover-letters/))

6. **Personal motivation should be concrete.** Dartmouth asks what first sparked the applicant’s interest. Georgetown asks for a specific reason for interest in the organisation while keeping the focus on contribution. This supports a sentence such as “I keep coming back to finance because I want to test whether the number deserves to be used,” when that sentence is grounded in an actual checking decision, rather than a bare claim that finance is a passion. ([Dartmouth](https://careerdesign.dartmouth.edu/resources/cover-letter-guide/); [Georgetown](https://careercenter.georgetown.edu/major-career-guides/resumes-cover-letters/))

7. **Human editing matters more as generic AI wording spreads.** Cui, Dias, and Ye studied approximately five million Freelancer applications. Their results show that AI access increased textual alignment and callbacks, but the relationship between tailoring and callbacks weakened after AI-generated letters became common; human editing was associated with better outcomes. UC Davis therefore recommends using AI as assistance while keeping the final application in the applicant’s authentic voice. ([Cui, Dias & Ye](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25054); [UC Davis AI guidance](https://careercenter.ucdavis.edu/resumes-and-materials/cover-letters))

8. **Tailoring is useful only when it creates information.** iHire reports that 77.3% of surveyed hiring professionals preferred a customised cover letter, but customisation must explain something specific about the fit. Jobscan’s large application analysis likewise frames the cover letter’s value as context around how the applicant’s experience maps to the employer’s problem. These are practitioner or survey sources, not causal proof; they support the direction, not a guaranteed result. ([iHire](https://www.ihire.com/resourcecenter/employer/pages/the-value-of-a-cover-letter-survey); [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter/))

## Diagnosis of the ING draft

The ING draft was factually strong and ATS-safe, but it still sounded polished because:

- The opener paraphrased the job description (“analyse FX data,” “reports and dashboards,” “sales, trading, and technology”) before giving the reader a reason Kien personally cares about that work.
- The body used a résumé sequence: FLC → model → sensitivity → SQL; BIDV → reports → profiles → tools. The facts were true, but the reader never saw the moment that forced a choice.
- The most human FLC material, the 64% IRR that looked implausible and the floating-rate risk that Kien raised before bank submission, was either omitted or reduced to a process description.
- The thesis (“financial data is useful when…”) was a polished summary. A stronger thesis states Kien’s working rule in first person: what he notices, what he checks, and what makes him useful.
- The close named skills and a generic contribution. It should name the judgement ING would see on day one.

## Story-depth model for Kien

For each letter, choose one primary story and one supporting proof point. Build the primary story with these fields:

1. **Moment:** What exactly happened? Use the concrete number, assumption, request, or deadline.
2. **Signal:** What made Kien pause? What looked wrong, incomplete, or easy to misread?
3. **Choice:** What did he decide to check, question, or explain first?
4. **Action:** What did he actually do, using only supported tools and process details?
5. **Consequence:** What changed, what meeting or decision did it reach, or what was prevented? If the consequence is unknown, say only what is known and leave the result open.
6. **Working rule:** What does the moment reveal about how he works?
7. **Day-one transfer:** Where would that same judgment appear in the target role?

The supporting proof point should be shorter. It can carry a metric or tool, but it should support the primary story rather than create a second list.

## Applied to Kien’s confirmed finance stories

- **64% IRR:** Kien calculated a 64% IRR, recognised it was implausible against his stated 18–25% comparable range, and sanity-checked it with colleagues. The correction and final decision are unknown, so the letter may show the pause and the check but may not claim a corrected number or project outcome.
- **Floating-rate risk:** Kien noticed a bank’s quarterly floating rate could spike based on the previous year’s trend, tested the effect on sensitivity analysis, and raised it at an internal board meeting before documents went to the bank. The later decision is unknown, so the letter may claim the observation, test, and escalation only.
- **Finance motivation:** Show the motivation through these choices. Avoid “finance is my passion” unless Kien adds a specific explanation in his own words.

## Operating rule for future letters

Before drafting, write a one-line answer to: **What would the reader remember about how Kien works after the letter is closed?** If the answer is a list of tools or employers, the draft is still a CV. If it is a judgement (“he stops when the number does not make sense, checks the assumption, and makes the risk visible before the decision”), the letter has a human centre.

## Sources inventory

The linked sources above are the evidence base for the framework. They include university career centres, government career guidance, professional surveys, practitioner guidance, and one large-scale academic study. University and government examples teach structure; they do not prove that the sample itself caused a hire. The Cui, Dias, and Ye paper is the strongest outcome evidence in this set, but it studies Freelancer applications rather than ING applications, so it should be treated as directional rather than deterministic.

## Why GPTZero may still flag a human-edited letter

A detector score is a probability estimate, not proof of authorship. GPTZero says its current system uses a deep-learning classifier trained to identify text that resembles language-model output; its older public explanations also describe statistical signals such as predictability and sentence variation. ([GPTZero methodology](https://gpt-zero.com/methodology); [GPTZero support](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/9585228410-how-do-i-interpret-burstiness-or-perplexity))

A cover letter can trigger those signals even when the facts are genuine because application prose is short, grammatical, formal, and deliberately organised. A 2024 study of AI detectors found that GPTZero and other systems can misclassify human writing, and a Frontiers study concluded that detectors should not be used as the only measure of authorship. ([International Journal for Educational Integrity](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-023-00140-5); [Frontiers in Education](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2024.1374889))

The practical response is not to add random errors or try to evade a detector. It is to make the writing independently verifiable as Kien's: use his unusual moments, his own causal wording, his uncertainty where it is real, and his actual explanation of why a number made him stop. Keep drafts, notes, and the source CV. The detector score should be treated as a style warning, not as a truth test.

## ING event hook found for this application

The earlier ING drafts used only the job-description hook. Official ING material provides stronger, company-specific context:

- In 2018, ING and Dutch pension fund PGGM co-created **Katana Lens**, a web application using predictive analytics on hundreds of thousands of trades to help investors compare trade ideas in minutes. ING described the problem as investors relying on assumptions to narrow millions of ideas, which was slow and could lead to missed opportunities. ([ING: AI tool sharpens investor decisions on bonds](https://ing.com/news/2020/07/ai-tool-sharpens-investor-decisions-on-bonds.html))
- ING's **FX Talking June 2026** report turns current FX data into forecasts across developed, emerging, and Asian currency pairs. It is a concrete example of the same data-to-decision movement the eFX Sales role asks an intern to support. ([ING Think: FX Talking June 2026](https://think.ing.com/uploads/pdf-replacements/FXT_Final_June_LS_HO_RB.pdf))
- ING's 2025 Form 20-F describes a client-driven Financial Markets franchise serving clients with multiple market and trading products. ([ING 2025 Form 20-F](https://ing.com/binaries/content/assets/documents/annual-reports/2025-ing-groep-nv-form-20-f.pdf))

For the letter, use one of these facts as the opener and diagnose the problem in plain language. The Katana Lens example is the strongest narrative hook because it names the project, the partner, the data problem, and the practical outcome. State clearly that it is an adjacent Financial Markets example rather than claiming it was an eFX project.

## What the screenshot reveals about the ING letter

The 100% GPTZero result should not be read as proof that the text was generated. Short application prose is a difficult classification setting, and studies report false positives. The result is still useful as an editing prompt because this draft contains several predictable signals:

- Most sentences are complete, evenly balanced, and similar in length.
- Paragraphs begin with a visible template transition: “At FLC Group,” then “At BIDV,” then a thesis and a value close.
- Abstract nouns carry the argument: “opportunities,” “assumption,” “information,” “attention,” “analysis,” and “habit.”
- The letter explains its own fit in polished bridge sentences instead of letting the events show it.
- It uses parallel groups such as currency pairs and tool or task lists. These are factually correct, but repeated list rhythm can look generated.
- The human moments are present but compressed. The reader hears the 64% number and the board meeting, but not enough of Kien’s immediate thought: “I did not know what had created the gap.”

The revision should keep the unusual facts, shorten the explanation around them, and let one personal reaction carry the paragraph. Do not add fake typos, slang, or invented emotion to lower a detector score. The goal is independently explainable writing that Kien can defend in an interview.

## Follow-up review after the second GPTZero scan

The second scan still classified the letter as AI-generated. I am treating that as a failed style target for this workflow, even though detector research shows that the score is not an authorship finding. GPTZero says its current detector is a deep-learning classifier trained to recognise text that resembles language-model output, and its own support documentation says the older perplexity and burstiness explanation no longer describes the current model. ([GPTZero technology](https://gptzero.me/technology); [GPTZero support](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/9585228410-how-do-i-interpret-burstiness-or-perplexity))

The practical signals we missed were visible in the draft itself:

- The first paragraph used a staged reaction (“The part I kept coming back to...”) followed by a bridge (“That is the part... and it is why...”). This explains the writing's meaning before the story has earned it.
- The body used a tidy two-case pattern: FLC story, then BIDV story, followed by a direct “same habit” transfer sentence. The facts were specific, but the paragraph shape was reusable.
- The draft ended with a deliberately compressed working rule and a clean contribution sentence. Both were valid, but the rhythm was too controlled when combined with the other signals.
- The prose was fully grammatical, short, and evenly organised. That is normal for an application, but it removes the unevenness visible in Kien's own HEINEKEN sample.

Additional research confirms two cautions relevant to Kien's writing. Liang et al. found that GPT detectors consistently misclassified non-native English writing as AI-generated more often than native writing. ([Patterns / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819)) A recent review of detection research describes human writing as more variable in sentence length, structure, and word choice, while also stressing that detector results are affected by text length and genre. ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12752165/))

The correction is therefore a voice edit, not a detector hack: start with the real ING report, let Kien's reaction arrive in his own words, keep the 64% IRR and floating-rate decisions, remove the explanatory bridges that could be pasted into another letter, and use one plain working rule. No invented mistakes, slang, or random wording should be added.

## Expanded example review: what the flow actually looks like

I reviewed more than twenty published internship, finance, consulting, and “this letter got me hired” examples. The corpus included the University of Maryland internship sample, the Notre Dame MBA student samples, UVA's business internship sample set, Princeton's consulting example, Columbia's microfinance letter, Yeshiva's finance internship example, Penn State's internship letters, Internshala's first-person DRDO success letter, and published letters by Brian Shoicket, Cristina Parvu, Zachary Galia, and Bayode Iranola. ([UMD examples](https://careers.umd.edu/find-jobs-internships/resumes-cover-letters/cover-letter-resources); [Notre Dame samples](https://mendoza-mbacareers.nd.edu/assets/557894/cover_letter_guide_and_student_samples_24_25_final.pdf); [UVA samples](https://career.virginia.edu/resources/cover-letter-examples-templates/); [Princeton consulting sample](https://spia.princeton.edu/sites/default/files/content/CovConsulting1.pdf); [Columbia microfinance sample](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/tamer_center/MicrofinanceCoverResume.pdf); [Yeshiva sample](https://www.yu.edu/sites/default/files/legacy/uploadedFiles/Student_Life/Resources_and_Services/Career_Development_Center/Students/Tips_and_Resources/COVER%20LETTERS.pdf); [Penn State examples](https://courses.ems.psu.edu/styleforstudents/sites/styleforstudents/files/file/chapter%209/Letters1_new.pdf); [Internshala success example](https://internshala.com/blog/how-i-got-an-internship-in-drdo-internship-cover-letter/); [Shoicket letter](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/cover-letter-got-me-job-3-years-ago-even-though-didnt-brian-shoicket); [Parvu letter](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20141105023754-2126623-cover-letters-matter-more-than-you-think); [Galia letter](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/apply-creative-jobs-creatively-zachary-galia); [Iranola letter guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/cover-letter-got-me-hired-how-write-yours-bayode-iranola-mjjle))

The repeated flow in the more memorable examples is:

1. **A real starting point.** The writer begins with meeting someone, seeing a project, noticing a problem, or remembering a specific moment. The company and role appear in the first paragraph, but the sentence is not a generic “I am excited to apply” statement.
2. **A short scene with a choice.** The writer says what happened and what they decided to do. The choice is often ordinary: check the number, ask a question, send the email, test an assumption, or make something clearer.
3. **One supporting proof.** A second example is shorter and confirms the same working habit. It is not a second résumé paragraph.
4. **A direct reason for the application.** The writer says what they want to do in this role in ordinary language. They do not need to explain the entire lesson of the letter.
5. **A plain close.** “I would like to talk about...” is enough when it follows a specific story. The examples that felt most human did not add a universal principle after every story.

The examples also show what to stop doing. The polished templates repeatedly use “I am excited,” “I believe,” “aligns with,” “contribute meaningfully,” and a three-part list of skills. The successful first-person examples use names, places, small details, unusual numbers, direct verbs, and occasional sentences that are longer or less symmetrical than a template. Shoicket's letter starts with a real event and then explains the employer's voice in his own words; Internshala's letter explains the applicant's failed attempts and editing process before showing the experiment that made him memorable. Those letters work because the reader can picture the writer making choices, not because the prose is perfectly smooth.

## Repository review: three projects with the word “humanizer”

The three repositories solve different problems:

- **[blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer)** is an agent skill. Its useful rule is to treat the draft as material, mark structural tells first, preserve every supported fact, match a supplied writing sample, then run one final audit. The local `~/.codex/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md` is an exact byte-for-byte copy of the repository's current `SKILL.md`, so installing it again would add nothing. ([Repository README](https://github.com/blader/humanizer); [skill source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blader/humanizer/main/SKILL.md))
- **[Humanizr/Humanizer](https://github.com/Humanizr/Humanizer)** is a .NET library for deterministic string, date, duration, number, quantity, pluralization, and culture formatting. It is useful for product code that needs readable output, but it is not an AI-writing editor and has no cover-letter workflow. ([README](https://github.com/Humanizr/Humanizer))
- **[harshaneel/humanize](https://github.com/harshaneel/humanize)** bundles two static skills, `humanize` and `ai-check`. Its useful additions are a six-part writer-profile pass before rewriting, countable sentence-rhythm checks, an explicit RLHF or “helpful assistant” voice audit, a self-rewrite distance check, and a separate forensic report that quotes every fired signal. ([humanize skill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/harshaneel/humanize/main/humanize/SKILL.md); [ai-check skill](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/harshaneel/humanize/main/ai-check/SKILL.md))

For Kien's application letters, the important lesson is to borrow the process rather than blindly copy numerical thresholds. Writer-profile extraction should record sentence rhythm, vocabulary level, paragraph openers, punctuation, recurring phrases, and transition habits before drafting. The final audit should check predictability, repeated medium-length sentences, structural scaffolding, generic transitions, and “helpful assistant” language. A forced six-word fragment or an artificial typo would make a formal ING application worse, so rhythm checks are diagnostics and facts remain the highest priority.

The third repository also documents a ceiling that matters here: a static rewrite alone cannot guarantee a low score from a learned commercial classifier. Its recommended hybrid steps are an independent second-model review, a self-rewrite-distance check, and, where a real detector is available, comparing several independently drafted variants. These steps must never alter a metric, add an experience, or optimise by inserting errors. The cover-letter workflow now treats them as optional QA methods, not as permission to manufacture a voice.

## Consequence for the Kien workflow

The previous rule requiring a thesis line in every letter was too rigid. A thesis is now optional. Keep it only when Kien naturally expresses a working rule in the source material; otherwise let the final concrete sentence do the work. The workflow must choose between three shapes based on the evidence: (a) one story plus one supporting proof, (b) two linked decisions without a summary thesis, or (c) the calibration-style thesis when Kien himself has supplied that wording or the story clearly earns it. A letter must not contain a company hook, a staged reaction, a shared-challenge bridge, two symmetrical proof paragraphs, a thesis, and a generic CTA all at once.

## Anti-slop repository review: stop-slop, taste-skill, and no-ai-slop

The follow-up repository review added three useful checks and one boundary:

- **[hardikpandya/stop-slop](https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop)** adds a minimum, practical pass: cut filler and adverb emphasis, remove binary contrasts and dramatic fragmentation, name the human actor instead of giving agency to data or decisions, avoid narrator-from-a-distance prose, and score directness, rhythm, trust, authenticity, and density. Its portability test is useful for cover letters: a sentence that could move unchanged to another company is likely filler.
- **[petergyang/no-ai-slop](https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop)** reinforces minimum effective editing. Keep strong human sentences, preserve useful edge and uncertainty, remove faux-insight setups, colon reveals, interpretive metadiscourse, synonym cycling, fake-strong verbs, and summary kickers. It also requires checking the edited draft against an independent evaluation list rather than polishing every sentence to the same level.
- **[Leonxlnx/taste-skill](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill)** is a frontend design skill, not a writing humanizer. Its transferable idea is “read the room before using defaults”: infer the audience, register, constraints, and existing style before choosing a treatment. Its design-specific dials, GSAP rules, layout checks, and anti-UI-slop rules do not belong in a cover-letter workflow.

The missing safeguards were therefore added to `humanizer`: actor and agency checks, vague-declarative checks, adverb and filler checks, the portability test, minimum effective editing, and preservation of strong sentences. The formal application mode now performs a factual lock, a literal count gate, one Signal I audit loop, and a stop condition when another pass would only create synonym swaps.
